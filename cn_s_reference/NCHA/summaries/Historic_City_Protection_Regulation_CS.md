# Regulation on famous historic cities, towns, and villages (历史文化名城名镇名村保护条例)

**Architect critical summary for schematic design**

State Council Order No. 524, in force 2008-07-01. Confirm whether a later consolidation changes an article before citing one. | State Council; housing and cultural-relics authorities share supervision

> Scope note: This is an architect's digest for routing and early design. It is not the text of the law or standard. Confirm every number, clause, and edition in the official publication before design, tender, or submission.

## Regulatory Overview

This regulation covers a designated famous historic city, town, or village, including its historic districts. A listed cultural-relics site still follows the Cultural Relics Protection Law. A locally listed historic building may also follow a city rule. This digest does not merge those regimes.

## Critical topics

- Ask whether the site is inside a protection boundary, a core protection area, or a construction-control zone. A 控规 envelope is not that boundary.
- The protection plan sets intensity and construction control. Massing that meets plot ratio and breaks the historic scale is still a problem.
- In a core protection area, new build and extension are not the ordinary planning path. Necessary infrastructure and public service are the narrow exception, and the cultural-relics authority is consulted before the planning permit.
- Historic buildings in the core keep their height, bulk, appearance, and colour unless the protection decision says otherwise.
- Demolition inside the core, other than of a historic building, needs both planning and cultural-relics approval. Do not draw a clearance scheme as the concept.
- Fire access that cannot meet the ordinary standard needs a specific fire-safety plan agreed with the fire authority and the planning authority. It is not a waiver the architect writes.

## Schematic-design takeaway

Stop the massing options until the protection plan and the boundary are on the table.
